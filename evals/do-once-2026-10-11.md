# Does a "do it once" rule in the writer prompt lift the outcome lever? (11 Oct night, agent, dev only)

Why: the baseline eval run and the read-and-count pass (docs/evals/read-2026-10-11.md) found chapter 1s that explain and show but never have the reader do the thing (rubric r1, "delivers its outcome" L3). The ten hand-built handbooks, whose plans put a Try it card or a "Your turn" in every chapter, score L4.

How: convex/evalDoOnce.ts. For a test handbook that already has a plan and a research brief, chapter 1 is written twice on Gemini 3.8 Flash with the user message generateChapter sends: once with the live writer prompt (CHAPTER_PROMPT_V6), once with a "DO IT ONCE" paragraph appended. Each chapter is scored by the rubric scorer on outcome and facts. Two runs per arm. Nothing stored but the call log; the live prompt untouched.

| Topic (plan from the baseline run) | Live writer | With "do it once" |
|---|---|---|
| Chess with your nephew | outcome 3, facts 4 · outcome 3, facts 4 | outcome 3, facts 2 · outcome 3, facts 2 |
| Starting your first SIP | outcome 4, facts 2 (with a doing card) · error | outcome 4, facts 2 · outcome 4, facts 2 (both with a doing card) |
| Making dal tadka | outcome 2, facts 4 · outcome 3, facts 4 | outcome 3, facts 4 · error |
| Compound interest, git | the comparison errored on the server | — |

Read: no lift on outcome where it mattered (chess stayed at 3), and on chess the facts score fell from 4 to 2 in both runs. Three topics and two runs are too few to say more than "no sign it helps". The writer follows the plan's blocks: when the plan gives no "try" or "tryit" block, the rule in the writer's prompt doesn't add one.

Decision (agent): no change to the writer. (A first guess was to move the rule into the plan; the second test below shows the live plan already does that, so the gap is elsewhere.)

## What the scorer actually objected to

The scorer's own reasons on the baseline's L3 outcome scores: mostly story recaps and explainers (Avengers, Doctor Doom, tides, "Jev's agent thing"), each "the reader never retells or explains it themselves". A story recap has no exercises by design (the story mode rule), so rubric r1's L4 ("the reader does it once") can't be reached by a recap at all. That's an edge case for the rubric, the fix Eval basics prescribes: rubric r2 counts a "say it back" or "tell it in your own words" prompt as doing it for a recap or an explainer (applied after the baseline run, so the run is scored on one rubric).

Note on r2: it does not lower the bar. It says what "doing it" means for a recap or an explainer (a retell or explain-it-yourself prompt). A recap with no such prompt still scores L3. Getting recaps to L4 needs one "say it back in a sentence" line in story-mode chapters, which today's story rule ("NO exercises at all") doesn't ask for: a writer change for Prateek to approve, judged by the gate.
