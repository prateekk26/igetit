# Hold a room for 10 minutes: the 28 days (draft)

## For future agent
Outline only, written by the agent from docs/public-speaking-plan.md for Prateek to approve (D65). Every title and line here is (agent) until Prateek rewrites it. Nothing is generated yet. Rules: the aha within 2 minutes of day 1, real value within 5; each day under 5 minutes; pictures and a Try it card where they help; Indian settings; no "car park". Value comes early: by day 7 the reader has given a real 2-minute talk; the rest stretches it to 10.

## One mode, with a worked example (Prateek, 10 Oct: "keep it one mode the way we were doing it earlier but walk them through a common example of how it can be done and they can follow along on their own")
Superseded the three-mode idea the same day. No mode screen, nothing typed or stored.
- Every day teaches the step, then shows it done on one running example: a sample speaker building their own introduction (week 1: the interview "Tell me about yourself" and the 20-second party or first-meeting version), then a short researched talk (weeks 3 and 4). The reader watches the same talk grow from day 1 to day 28.
- Each day ends with a "Your turn" card: do the same step for your own intro or topic, on paper or out loud. Optional; it never blocks the day.
- Why: the worked-example effect (beginners learn a skill faster from watching a step done than from being told it), and the generation effect for anyone who does their turn (Slamecka & Graf 1978). The Xunzi saying ("putting it into practice") is the spirit; learning styles are not used (Pashler 2008).
- Value on day 1: the example's first line of an intro, and theirs if they do their turn.

## Week 1: your first 2 minutes (the value, early)
1. **One idea they'll repeat.** Aha by card 3: a Try it card shows 3 openings for the same talk; tap the one a listener repeats at the chai break. Value: write your own one-sentence idea.
2. **Open with a promise.** The first 20 seconds decide if they listen. Write your opening line.
3. **Three stops, no more.** A 2-minute talk has three stops on the way to the idea. Lay out yours.
4. **Close on the line they'll repeat.** The last line is the one they carry home. Write it.
5. **Shaky hands are normal.** Why the body does it; say "I'm excited", not "I'm calm" (Brooks 2014); one slow breath out.
6. **Cues, not a script.** Five words on a card beat a memorised page. Make your cue card; say it once out loud (timer).
7. **Give your first 2 minutes.** To one person: a friend, family at dinner, a voice note. Ask them: "what line do you remember?" Pass when it matches your idea.

## Week 2: body and voice
8. **Stand still, hands free.** What to do with your hands, and why pacing reads as nerves.
9. **One person per thought.** Eye contact in a room of 5, 50 or 500.
10. **The pause is a tool.** Pace, and the 2-second pause after the important line.
11. **Reach the back row.** Volume without shouting.
12. **Basically, actually, na.** Filler words, the Indian English ones too, and how to swap them for a pause.
13. **When you blank.** The recovery line, and why the room rarely notices.
14. **Give 3 minutes, recorded.** Record on your phone; listening back is optional, with one thing to listen for.

## Week 3: something worth saying (research it, then present it)
15. **Start with the room.** Pick a topic for what your listeners care about, not what you know.
16. **Research it in 30 minutes.** 3 facts and 1 story; ask a person first, then PIB and RBI for Indian numbers; keep only what serves the idea.
17. **A story beats a statistic.** In a Stanford class, 63% remembered the stories and 5% any statistic.
18. **Make numbers land.** Compare to a cricket ground, a cup of chai, a Mumbai local at 9 AM.
19. **Slides that help, or none.** One idea a slide, or no slides at all.
20. **Explain the hard part simply.** One comparison the listener already knows.
21. **Give 5 minutes on what you researched.** To a small group or a team meeting; ask what line they remember.

## Week 4: hold the room for 10
22. **The 10-minute shape.** Three acts, with the idea stated at the start and the end.
23. **Reset attention every 2 minutes.** A question, a story, a show of hands.
24. **Questions from the room.** The hard one, the hostile one, and "I don't know, here's how I'll find out."
25. **Different rooms.** An interview panel, a team review, a wedding toast, a college fest.
26. **On a video call.** Camera at eye level, look at the lens on the key line, the mute-button moment.
27. **Rehearse like it counts.** Three run-throughs, one in front of a person, one standing.
28. **Hold a room for 10 minutes.** Give it. Ask the room what line they remember.
